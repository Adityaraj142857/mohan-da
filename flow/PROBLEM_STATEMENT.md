# Problem Statement: Automating Orders and Payment Verification for a Small Food Shop

## 1. Summary

A small food shop takes orders on WhatsApp, calculates totals by hand, collects payment by UPI QR, and verifies each payment by checking a relative's bank statement. Every order needs several manual steps, so the process is slow, error-prone and open to payment fraud. The owner wants to automate it at **zero or minimal cost**, **without raising prices**, and wants a way to **verify payment screenshots** that customers send.

---

## 2. Background

- The shop sells food items and offers two services:
  1. **Takeout**: the customer collects the order from the shop.
  2. **Delivery** to hostels: the shop delivers, and charges **₹15 extra**.
- Customers are mostly hostel residents. They order through **WhatsApp**.
- The shop is small: no ordering software, no payment gateway, no staff dedicated to order handling.

---

## 3. Current process (fully manual)

1. The customer sends the names of the food items on WhatsApp.
2. The owner looks up each price in the menu and adds them up.
3. The owner asks: "Takeout or delivery?"
4. For delivery, the owner adds ₹15 to the total.
5. The owner sends the total and a payment **QR code**.
6. The customer pays and sends a **screenshot** of the payment.
7. The owner checks a **relative's bank statement** to see whether the money actually arrived.
8. Only then does the owner start preparing or dispatching the order.

---

## 4. Problems with the current process

| # | Problem | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Manual price lookup and addition** for every order | Slow; arithmetic mistakes; inconsistent delivery-fee handling |
| P2 | **Back-and-forth questions** (takeout or delivery, which hostel, which room) | Many messages per order; customers wait |
| P3 | **Payment verification is manual**: screenshot, then bank statement | Slowest step; delays food preparation |
| P4 | **Screenshots are not proof of payment.** They can be edited or generated by fake-payment apps | Risk of handing over food for money that never arrived |
| P5 | **Verification depends on someone else's bank account** (a relative) | Owner needs the relative's cooperation and access; delays and privacy concerns |
| P6 | **Matching payments to orders is guesswork** when several orders have the same amount | Wrong or missed confirmations |
| P7 | **Does not scale**: every extra order adds the same manual work | Owner is overloaded at busy times; orders are missed or delayed |
| P8 | **No records**: orders and payments live in chat history | Hard to reconcile the day, track sales or spot repeat fraud |

---

## 5. Problem statement

> A small food shop needs to turn a chat-based, manual order-and-payment workflow into an automated one. The system must understand a customer's free-text WhatsApp order, price it correctly from the menu (adding ₹15 for delivery), collect payment through UPI, and **confirm payment reliably without the owner checking a bank statement by hand**. It must also check the payment screenshots customers send, while recognising that a screenshot alone cannot prove payment. All of this must run at **zero or minimal running cost** and **must not raise what customers pay**.

---

## 6. Goals

**Primary goals**
- G1. Turn a free-text message ("2 samosa and 1 chai, hostel B room 204") into a correct, priced order with minimal back-and-forth.
- G2. Apply the pricing rule automatically: menu prices, plus ₹15 only for delivery, and nothing else.
- G3. Send the customer a clear way to pay the exact amount.
- G4. **Confirm payments automatically** from real evidence (the bank's credit alert), not from a screenshot alone.
- G5. **Check screenshots** customers send: extract amount, reference number, payee and time; flag wrong, stale, duplicate or suspicious ones.
- G6. Give the owner one place to see paid orders, orders needing attention, and the day's totals.

**Secondary goals**
- G7. Keep a clean record of orders, payments and suspected fraud.
- G8. Work first as a **demo with no external accounts**, then connect to real WhatsApp with configuration changes only.
- G9. Let the owner keep control: the owner can approve, reject or override any payment decision.

---

## 7. Constraints

| # | Constraint |
|---|---|
| C1 | **Zero or minimal cost.** No payment-gateway fees, no paid hosting, no paid AI by default. |
| C2 | **No price increase for customers.** Delivery stays at ₹15. Any matching trick must not add to what customers pay. |
| C3 | **Non-technical owner.** Setup, running and daily use must be simple. |
| C4 | **Runs on the owner's own computer** (free tools only). |
| C5 | **Official WhatsApp channel only.** No unofficial WhatsApp automation that risks a ban. |
| C6 | **UPI payments to the owner's own UPI ID** (no merchant gateway). |
| C7 | **Data stays local** (no cloud database). |

---

## 8. Scope

**In scope**
- Understanding item orders in casual English, Hinglish and romanised Bengali.
- Menu, pricing and the ₹15 delivery rule.
- Takeout vs delivery, hostel and room capture.
- UPI payment link and QR for the exact amount, with expiry.
- Payment confirmation from bank credit alerts (SMS forwarded to the system).
- Screenshot checking (OCR) with fraud checks.
- Owner console with live order board, review queue and reports.
- Owner alerts (free channel).
- A chat simulator for demos and testing.
- Connection to WhatsApp (official Cloud API), first with the free test number.

**Out of scope (for now)**
- Payment-gateway integration (paid, needs KYC).
- Cash on delivery.
- Inventory management, loyalty programs, discounts.
- Rider or delivery-tracking app.
- Voice-note orders.
- Multiple shops.
- Accounting or tax filing.

| Who | What they need |
|---|---|
| **Customer** (hostel resident) | Order in a few messages, see the exact price, pay easily, get quick confirmation |
| **Shop owner** | Less manual work, trustworthy payment confirmation, clear view of orders |

## 10. Success criteria

*Targets below are proposals. The owner should confirm or change them.*

| Measure | Target |
|---|---|
| Orders that need **no** owner typing (price, total, payment prompt) | Most orders; the owner only handles exceptions |
| **Orders wrongly marked paid** | **Zero** |
| Time from customer's payment to confirmation message | Under about 1 minute when the bank alert arrives normally |
| Payment confirmed without checking a bank statement by hand | Every order where the bank alert is received |
| Order-parsing accuracy on a realistic test set | At least 95% |
| Screenshot field extraction (amount, reference number, status) on the owner's real screenshots | At least 90%, measured before trusting it |
| Extra cost to run per month | ₹0 in the demo; a small, known figure once real WhatsApp customers are added |
| Customers charged more than the menu total plus ₹15 delivery | **Never** |

## 11. Assumptions and dependencies

- The receiving bank sends an **SMS alert for every UPI credit**, and that SMS can be forwarded from the phone holding the SIM (with that person's consent).
- Customers pay through common UPI apps (GPay, PhonePe, Paytm, etc.) and have WhatsApp.
- The owner has a computer that can stay on during shop hours.
- Bank SMS formats differ by bank, so the system needs the owner's **real (redacted) sample messages** to be tuned.
- The owner can supply the real menu, hostel names, UPI ID and payee name.

---

## 12. Risks and open issues

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation in the design |
|---|---|---|
| Fake or edited screenshots | Money never arrives | Paid status comes only from a matched bank credit or an owner action |
| Bank SMS forwarder stops working | No automatic confirmation | Nothing is falsely marked paid; orders go to the owner for one-tap review; owner is alerted |
| OCR misreads real screenshots | Wrong extraction | Test on real screenshots first; bank credit remains the deciding evidence |
| Free WhatsApp is limited to 5 test recipients | Cannot serve real customers on the test number | Production needs a dedicated business number and setup with Meta; check current pricing before launch |
| WhatsApp message charges after the free allowance | Small recurring cost | Keep messages per order low; track the count; verify Meta's current pricing |
| Free tunnels change URL on restart | Webhook link must be re-entered | Acceptable for the demo; stable free options documented |
| Personal UPI ID used for business collection | Banks may notice high-volume use | Collection ID is one setting; can be swapped for a merchant QR later |
| Receiving account belongs to a relative | Consent, privacy, tax and record-keeping questions | Owner should decide who legitimately receives the money; see Section 13 |

---

## 13. Questions for the owner

1. Which bank receives the payments, and can the owner get that bank's credit SMS on a phone they control?
2. If a relative's account stays in use, will they consent to SMS forwarding from their phone?
3. What are the real menu, prices and hostel names?
4. What are typical daily order counts? (This decides whether WhatsApp's free message allowance is enough.)
5. Should the small "matching discount" (up to 30 paise per order, borne by the shop) be used, or is exact-rupee pricing preferred with more manual review?
6. Should payments ever be accepted on screenshot alone for small orders? (The design says no by default.)
7. Is a phone-friendly web ordering page acceptable as an alternative to chat ordering? (It reduces WhatsApp message costs and parsing errors.)
